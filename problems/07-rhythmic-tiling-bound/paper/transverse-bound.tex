\begin{abstract}
We give a short second-moment proof that a same-color nonattacking placement of $t$ queens on a $k\times k$ board satisfies $t^2-1\leq 3k(k-t)$. Equivalently, this bounds a rainbow packing of three-term arithmetic progressions that each meet three consecutive length-$k$ blocks once. The proof and its application to actual exact-cover rhythmic tilings have been checked in Lean 4. The result is a supporting obstruction: it does not establish existence of perfect rhythmic triplet tilings for all sufficiently large orders. Novelty remains unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and relation to rhythmic tilings}
A perfect rhythmic triplet tiling of order $k$ partitions $\{0,\ldots,3k-1\}$ into $k$ sets $\{a_i,a_i+d_i,a_i+2d_i\}$ with positive, pairwise distinct integer gaps $d_i$. Translation by $1$ gives the equivalent convention $\{1,\ldots,3k\}$. A triplet is \emph{transverse} if it meets each consecutive length-$k$ block once.

\begin{theorem}[Partial transverse packing bound]\label{thm:main}
Let $a,b,c:\{1,\ldots,t\}\to\Z$ be individually injective, with values in $\U$, and suppose
\[
a_i+c_i=2b_i,\qquad a_i-c_i\ne a_j-c_j\quad(i\ne j).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bound}
t^2-1\leq 3k(k-t).
\end{equation}
Consequently \eqref{eq:bound} holds for the number $t$ of transverse triplets in any perfect rhythmic triplet tiling of order $k$.
\end{theorem}
The core statement does not assume that the partial packing extends to a perfect tiling. To pass from a tiling to this statement, subtract $k$ from every transverse middle term and $2k$ from every transverse last term. Exact coverage gives the three injective coordinate lists; the original gap is $k+(c_i-a_i)/2$.

\begin{theorem}[One-color queens bound]\label{thm:queens}
Let $(a_i,c_i)\in\U^2$, $1\leq i\leq t$, be mutually nonattacking queens, all on the same checkerboard color. Thus the four lists $a_i,c_i,a_i+c_i,a_i-c_i$ are individually injective, and $a_i+c_i$ has a common parity. Then \eqref{eq:bound} holds.
\end{theorem}
For even $a_i+c_i$, set $b_i=(a_i+c_i)/2$. This is a bijective correspondence between these queen placements and partial transverse rainbow triplet packings, via
\[
(a_i,c_i)\longleftrightarrow
\bigl(a_i,\ k+(a_i+c_i)/2,\ 2k+c_i\bigr).
\]
Rows and columns encode endpoint disjointness; the two diagonal directions encode distinct middle terms and gaps. These are partial packings, not necessarily completable tilings. The queens theorem also covers odd parity directly.

\section{Proof by second moments}
\begin{lemma}[Spaced-list variance]\label{lem:variance}
If $z_1<\cdots<z_m$ are real numbers with $z_{i+1}-z_i\geq q\geq0$, then
\[
12\sum_{i=1}^m z_i^2\ \geq\ q^2m(m^2-1).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The assertion is immediate for $m=0$. Otherwise,
\begin{align*}
m\sum_i z_i^2-\left(\sum_i z_i\right)^2
&=\sum_{i<j}(z_j-z_i)^2\\
&\geq q^2\sum_{i<j}(j-i)^2
=\frac{q^2m^2(m^2-1)}{12}.
\end{align*}
Discard the nonnegative square and divide by $m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:queens}]
Injectivity of the row coordinates gives $t\leq k$; write $r=k-t$ and $h=(k-1)/2$. Let
\[
X_i=a_i-h,\quad Y_i=c_i-h,\quad U_i=X_i+Y_i,\quad V_i=X_i-Y_i.
\]
The $U_i$ are distinct and have pairwise differences in $2\Z$, and the same is true of the $V_i$. Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:variance} with spacing $2$ after sorting gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diag}
\sum_i U_i^2+\sum_i V_i^2\geq \frac{2t(t^2-1)}{3}.
\end{equation}
The full centered coordinate list has square sum
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(j-h)^2=\frac{k(k^2-1)}{12}.
\]
The $r$ omitted row coordinates are distinct integers translated by $h$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:variance}, with spacing $1$, bounds their square sum below by $r(r^2-1)/12$. Therefore
\[
\sum_i X_i^2\leq \frac{k(k^2-1)-r(r^2-1)}{12},
\]
and the same bound holds for $\sum_iY_i^2$. Since $U_i^2+V_i^2=2(X_i^2+Y_i^2)$, combining with \eqref{eq:diag} yields
\[
2t(t^2-1)\leq k(k^2-1)-r(r^2-1).
\]
If $t>0$, substitute $r=k-t$, factor out $t$, and simplify to \eqref{eq:bound}. If $t=0$, the required inequality is $-1\leq3k^2$.
\end{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} follows from the even-parity queens correspondence, and its tiling consequence follows from the coordinate restriction described above.

\begin{corollary}[Asymptotic consequence]
For $k>0$,
\[
t\leq\frac{\sqrt{21k^2+4}-3k}{2}.
\]
In particular, the limiting upper density of such queen placements or transverse partial packings is at most
\[
\frac{\sqrt{21}-3}{2}=0.7912878474\ldots.
\]
Every sequence of perfect rhythmic triplet tilings with orders tending to infinity therefore has nontransverse proportion with lower limit at least
\[
\frac{5-\sqrt{21}}{2}=0.2087121525\ldots.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Solve $t^2+3kt-3k^2-1\leq0$ for the nonnegative root, divide by $k$, and take the limit. For the tiling statement the proportion is $1-t/k$.
\end{proof}

The final theorems contain no assumed variance bound or assumed packing estimate:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=3pt]
\item \texttt{Rhythmic.partial\_transverse\_bound}: the actual injective-array bound. The formal core needs bounds only on $a,c$; midpoint bounds follow in the unshifted case.
\item \texttt{Rhythmic.PerfectTripletTiling.transverse\_bound}: the number of transverse triplets in a genuine exact-cover tiling.
\item \texttt{Rhythmic.monochromatic\_queens\_bound}: the bound from bounded integer row/column coordinates, four injectivity conditions, and a common sum remainder modulo $2$.
\end{itemize}
An exact-cover tiling is represented by positive distinct gaps together with a bijection
\[
\operatorname{Fin}(k)\times\operatorname{Fin}(3)\simeq\operatorname{Fin}(3k)
\]
whose value is the specified start plus the term index times the gap. Thus the formal tiling theorem concerns an actual partition, rather than merely a numerical condition.

All inequalities are stated using exact casts into $\R$, with the subtraction $k-t$ taken there rather than truncated natural-number subtraction. The displayed integer inequality is equivalent. The square-root reformulation and limiting constants above are mathematical corollaries; they are not separately formalized. Lean's axiom audit reports only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}; there are no proof placeholders or added axioms. \section{Prior art and limitations}
The exact eventual-existence question for perfect rhythmic triplet tilings is explicitly left open in the 2026 packing papers~\cite{alon,mao}. The July paper establishes an asymptotic prescribed-gap packing result, not an exact tiling theorem. Johnson's original account gives examples and elementary restrictions~\cite{johnson}; tiling counts are catalogued in OEIS A261516~\cite{tilingoeis}.

The partial transverse extremal problem is already the one-color queens problem in OEIS A352241~\cite{queensoeis}. Its linked 2022 discussion~\cite{queensmse} contains computational counterexamples to a proposed bound. A 2025 discussion~\cite{queenspuzzle} gives an obstruction to placing $k-1$ same-color queens on larger boards and constructions with asymptotic density $2/3$; it does not state the bound proved here. A targeted search found no instance of \eqref{eq:bound}, but that does not establish novelty. In particular, the full text of Davalan's substantial treatment~\cite{davalan} was not available for review, and a linked Greek forum discussion could not be retrieved. The variance argument is elementary enough that an equivalent result may be known under other terminology.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Unresolved main objective.} Nothing here proves that every sufficiently large $k$ admits a perfect rhythmic triplet tiling. Computational witnesses, unsuccessful construction searches, and specialized inflation obstructions remain research checkpoints, not substitutes for that existence theorem. No publication or external submission has been made.
